TITLE: Earthquake Response Guidelines

Earthquakes cause structural collapse, secondary fires, and trapped or injured people. Aftershocks are common and can cause further collapse of already-weakened structures.

Immediate priorities:
- Assume all structures near the epicenter may be structurally compromised; discourage re-entry into damaged buildings until inspected.
- Identify trapped or injured people through visual and verbal checks in accessible collapsed areas; do not send untrained personnel into unstable rubble.
- Watch for secondary hazards: gas leaks, downed power lines, ruptured water mains, and aftershocks.
- Establish a safe assembly point in open ground away from buildings, trees, and power lines.

Medical considerations:
- Crush injuries, fractures, lacerations, and head trauma are the most common injuries from structural collapse.
- Crush syndrome (from prolonged limb compression) can cause life-threatening complications once pressure is released; extraction should be coordinated with medical personnel when possible.
- Triage should prioritize suspected spinal or head injuries, uncontrolled bleeding, and people trapped for extended periods.

Search and rescue:
- Light search and rescue can be performed by trained bystanders using a "shout, listen, tap" method around rubble.
- Heavy rescue requiring lifting equipment should be left to specialized teams to avoid further collapse.

Shelter and resources:
- Displaced residents should be moved to open-air or reinforced shelter locations, not damaged buildings.
- Stockpile water, food, blankets, and lighting since utilities are frequently disrupted for days.
- Coordinate structural engineers or safety inspectors before allowing re-entry to assess buildings.

Source: General earthquake-response best practices, condensed for decision-support purposes.
