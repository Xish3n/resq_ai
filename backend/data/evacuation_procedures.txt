TITLE: General Evacuation Procedures

Effective evacuation depends on clear routes, prioritized movement order, and accountability for who has left the area.

Planning priorities:
- Identify the hazard's direction of spread (water flow, fire front, structural collapse zone) before choosing an evacuation route.
- Choose routes that move people away from the hazard without crossing through it; the shortest route is not always the safest one.
- Designate a primary and a backup route in case the primary becomes blocked.

Order of movement:
- Prioritize people who cannot self-evacuate: those with mobility limitations, injuries, young children, and elderly residents.
- Medical cases requiring stabilization should be evacuated with trained personnel accompanying them when possible.
- Able-bodied individuals can often assist with guiding groups or carrying supplies, freeing responders for higher-need cases.

Accountability and communication:
- Maintain a headcount or registration point at the assembly area to confirm who has evacuated safely.
- Use clear, simple, repeated messaging about the evacuation route and destination; avoid technical jargon that could cause confusion or panic.
- Designate a single point of contact for updates to avoid conflicting instructions.

Shelter arrival:
- On arrival, separate people needing medical triage from the general population.
- Provide clear signage or verbal direction for water, sanitation, and rest areas.
- Track vulnerable individuals (unaccompanied minors, elderly, disabled) for follow-up support.
