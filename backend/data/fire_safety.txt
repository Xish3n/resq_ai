TITLE: Fire Emergency Response Guidelines

Structural and wildland fires spread quickly and create smoke inhalation risk well beyond the visible flame front.

Immediate priorities:
- Evacuate the immediate area and any structures in the fire's projected path before addressing anything else.
- Move people away from smoke plumes; smoke inhalation is often more dangerous than the flames themselves.
- Establish a perimeter and upwind assembly point so responders and evacuees are not exposed to shifting smoke.
- Do not allow re-entry into a burning or smoke-filled structure to retrieve belongings.

Medical considerations:
- Smoke inhalation, burns, and carbon monoxide exposure are the primary medical concerns.
- Triage should prioritize airway compromise, burns covering large body surface areas, and unconsciousness.
- People with asthma or respiratory conditions are at significantly elevated risk from smoke exposure.

Firefighting and containment:
- Untrained bystanders should not attempt interior firefighting; exterior containment (removing fuel, creating breaks) may be appropriate only if safe and trained.
- Wildland fires require monitoring of wind direction and speed, since these determine spread rate and evacuation urgency.

Evacuation and shelter:
- Evacuation routes should be chosen perpendicular to wind direction and away from the fire's path, not simply "away from the flames" which can lead into the smoke plume.
- Shelters should be located well outside the projected fire perimeter and have independent water/power where possible.

Resource management:
- Prioritize respiratory support (masks, oxygen if available), burn care supplies, and clean water.
- Coordinate with fire services for containment status updates before adjusting evacuation zones.
