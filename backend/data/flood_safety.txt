TITLE: Flood Safety and Response Guidelines

Flooding is one of the most common and destructive natural hazards. When heavy flooding affects an area, responders should prioritize life safety over property.

Immediate priorities:
- Move stranded people to higher ground or stable elevated structures before water levels rise further.
- Treat any moving water above knee height as dangerous; six inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet.
- Never attempt to drive or walk through flood water of unknown depth; vehicles can be swept away in as little as two feet of moving water.
- Assume flood water is contaminated with sewage, chemicals, or debris; avoid direct contact and provide clean water where possible.
- Cut electrical power to affected buildings if it is safe to do so, since submerged electrical systems create electrocution risk.

Medical considerations:
- Waterborne illness, hypothermia, and injuries from debris are the most common medical issues after flooding.
- Triage should prioritize people with breathing difficulty, suspected drowning/near-drowning, hypothermia, and open wounds exposed to contaminated water.
- People with chronic conditions (diabetes, dialysis, respiratory illness) are at elevated risk if medication or equipment access is disrupted.

Evacuation and shelter:
- Establish evacuation routes away from waterways and low-lying areas, and toward pre-identified shelters on high ground.
- Boats and high-clearance vehicles may be required once road-level flooding exceeds passable depth.
- Shelters should provide dry bedding, clean water, sanitation, and separate space for medical triage.

Resource management:
- Prioritize clean drinking water, food, blankets, and first-aid supplies for the first 24-48 hours.
- Coordinate with local emergency management and utility providers for pump-out and structural safety assessment before allowing re-entry.

Source: General flood-response best practices (FEMA/Red Cross style guidance), condensed for decision-support purposes.
